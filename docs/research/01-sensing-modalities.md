# Table 1 — Sensing modalities

Question this table answers: which sensor class can support residential **stud plumb QA** at about **0.12°** (derived from 1/4 inch in 10 feet; see [../tolerances.md](../tolerances.md)), i.e. a few millimeters of tip offset on an 8 foot stud.

**Short comparison.** High-end terrestrial laser scanners publish millimeter 3D accuracy and arcsecond-class angular specs at 10 m (vendor claims). Phone LiDAR publications found in this pass are **centimeter-class**. Robotics LiDAR added below (Livox, Hesai PandarXT, Ouster OS1, Velodyne VLP-16) publishes centimeter ranging, not a stud-plumb test. UWB and TI mmWave reference designs are **centimeter to decimeter** presence/ranging sensors, not edge metrology. XREAL Aura’s official page describes tracking cameras and a display, not a survey scanner. Photogrammetry accuracy is scene- and control-dependent; no stud-plumb spec was found. None of these rows is a measured OpenWall error budget.

Prior internal claim of **±3–10 mm RMSE at 1.5–2 m** for phone LiDAR: **unverified** this pass. Do not use it as a number.

## How a ranging spec becomes an angle

Industry plumb used here is **1/4 inch in 10 feet ≈ 0.1194°**, about **5.1 mm** of tip offset on an 8 ft stud. Both figures are **derived** in [../tolerances.md](../tolerances.md). An 8 ft stud is **2438.4 mm**.

`atan(ε / 2438.4 mm)` is the angular size of a single ranging error ε if one end of the stud moved by ε and the other end did not. Call that a **derived single-point equivalent**. It is not the standard error of a fitted stud axis. Uncorrelated noise can average down as points are added. A bias that changes from the top of the stud to the bottom does not. No row below counts points on a 2×4 or measures that bias on wood. Datasheet angular precision is the direction of a ray or of the instrument, not the angle of a fitted stud, unless the page says so.

### Decision for OpenWall

**Viable class: survey tripod TLS (S15), FARO Focus Premium or Leica RTC360.** Published 3D accuracy is about 2 mm at 10 m (derived single-point equivalent about 0.05°), and published angular accuracy is 18–19 arcsec (derived tip well under 1 mm on an 8 ft stud). SCENE or Cyclone can keep that level from the inclinometer or dual-axis compensator. Good registration is still required. This is not a measured stud error on lumber.

**Lowest price that meets the error is not the robotics LiDAR.** Mid-360 at a 2023 trial price of $749, and peers through a few thousand dollars, publish about 2 cm range precision. That is a derived ~0.47° if the 2 cm lands as a top-to-bottom bias. Ouster OS1 is tighter (±1.25 cm Lambertian on the Rev8 sheet) and still about 0.29° on the same derivation. Do not assume averaging will close that gap. It was not measured here.

**Skip the cheaper tripod scanner for the plumb call.** BLK360 G2 (S14) is the tempting middle. Its 4 mm at 10 m would be inside the tip budget, and a shop extract showed about $26,500 (live page unverified). The same manual specifies IMU tilt of **8 arcmin**, derived **0.133°** and about **5.7 mm** over 8 ft, which misses 0.12°.

**Also skip** phone LiDAR, UWB, mmWave, VLP-16, and handheld SLAM as the instrument that paints red/green. Phone and SLAM remain useful for rough capture and for learning a segmenter. SLAM “6 mm relative” is not a gravity-plumb spec, and the Horizon vendor figures in the two sources above do not even match.

**Dollars to treat as order-of-magnitude only.** RTC360 LT at **$68,096** is a 2026-09-23 shop extract, not a cart this pass opened. Focus Premium at **$41,860** is a quotation dated 2023-04-24 that expired 2023-09-30. Current Focus street price is **unverified**. Among instruments whose specs meet the tolerance, a current public list was not loaded live. The class conclusion does not depend on those dollars: survey TLS meets the published error; the classes under it do not.
